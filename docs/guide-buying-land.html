Buying Land in Door County: A Due-Diligence Guide

Work from a defined proposed use. A parcel can look suitable in a photograph while leaving the most important access, siting and infrastructure questions unanswered.

Tom Reynolds website · Updated November 8, 2026

Identify what you are buying

Obtain the parcel identification, survey information and title material. Locate the municipality and confirm the applicable zoning authority. County tax maps are a starting reference, not a substitute for a legal boundary survey or title review.

Investigate easements, deed restrictions, frontage, road status and maintenance arrangements. A visible path or neighbor’s use does not establish your legal right to drive, build or install utilities across another property.

Test the proposed building plan

Ask about setbacks, minimum lot rules, shoreland or wetland constraints, soils and wastewater capacity. The usable building area can differ substantially from total acreage. Discuss required site investigation with the appropriate authority and qualified professionals before relying on a buildable description.

Confirm electric, water, wastewater and internet options at the proposed site. Obtain written estimates when possible. Utility service somewhere along the road does not establish extension costs, capacity or installation timing.

Prepare a complete project budget

Add investigation, survey or title work, access, clearing, excavation, drainage, utilities, design, approvals and construction to the purchase price. Use site-specific quotes as the project becomes clearer and retain a contingency for unresolved conditions.

The cost to build a home cannot be established from a generic Door County figure. If the proposed use is recreational, agricultural, commercial or a subdivision, verify that use specifically rather than assuming residential guidance answers it.

Questions worth answering

Where do I start a Door County parcel lookup?

Use the county tax-parcel resources, then confirm the legal description, boundaries and responsible municipality through the transaction professionals and authorities.

Does a soil test settle every building question?

No. Wastewater suitability is one part of the investigation; legal access, zoning, siting, utilities and other conditions still matter.
