Door County Land & Lots for Sale: Buildability Questions

An attractive parcel is not the same as a confirmed building site. Establish access, permitted use and the practical cost of making the property usable before comparing acreage prices.

Start with the parcel, not its postal address

Locate the tax parcel and identify the city, village or town. Mailing names, school areas and familiar community names do not establish zoning jurisdiction. Use county parcel resources and then confirm the applicable zoning office and local requirements.

Ask for survey and title information, legal access and recorded easements. A visible track, neighboring driveway or road frontage does not by itself settle year-round access, maintenance responsibility or access for the proposed use.

Buildability requires several answers

Investigate minimum lot and siting requirements, setbacks, wetlands, shoreland rules, soils and wastewater options. A parcel’s size does not tell you how much of it can be used for a house, driveway, outbuildings or the intended activity. Ask the appropriate professionals about actual test and permit needs.

Utility availability should be confirmed at the proposed building location. Obtain specific estimates for service extensions, electric, internet, water and wastewater rather than treating the presence of a utility along the road as a connection quote.

Compare total project cost and intended use

Add the land price to investigation, site work, access, utilities, design, approvals and construction allowances. Leave room for uncertainty. A generic cost-per-square-foot figure cannot price an unexamined Door County site.

Acreage, recreational land, farm land, waterfront lots and commercial land can involve different permitted uses and professional review. Discuss your intended use before assuming a residential search covers the right property or that a parcel is approved for hunting, division or rental development.

Questions worth answering

Does land listed as a building lot guarantee a permit?

No. Confirm the proposed use, site conditions and approvals for the actual parcel before relying on that description.

How do I compare two parcels with different acreage?

Compare legal access, usable building area, infrastructure, restrictions and total project cost as well as acreage and asking price.
