A condo search should begin with the ownership structure. Similar-looking units can have very different occupancy, management, financing and maintenance obligations.

Residential, resort and fractional interests

A residential condominium, a hotel or resort condominium, and a fractional interest are not interchangeable products. Confirm the legal interest offered, permitted owner occupancy and whether an operating or rental-management agreement comes with the purchase. Descriptive marketing labels are not enough.

If year-round occupancy is important, verify it in the governing documents and with the responsible authority. If rental use is important, review municipal and state requirements separately from association and management restrictions. Never assume that other advertised rentals establish permission for the unit.

Read the full cost picture

Association dues are one component. Request the current budget, reserve information, recent meeting records, insurance summary, assessment notices and relevant management agreements. Ask which utilities and maintenance tasks are included, which remain yours and which expenses can change.

Compare purchase prices only after accounting for ownership rights, usable dates, financing and costs. A lower asking price or lower advertised dues does not prove lower total ownership costs. Unknown amounts should stay unknown until supported by current documents.

Check financing before committing

Condo project eligibility can affect financing, particularly for hotel-like or resort projects. Ask your lender to review the specific project and your intended occupancy early. A preapproval for a buyer is not automatically an approval of every condominium project.

Bring the unit, association name, intended use and document package to Tom and the appropriate professionals. A shortlist can then distinguish verified facts from outstanding requests instead of relying on generalized claims that a development allows rentals.

Questions worth answering

Can I live year-round in a Door County resort condo?

Not necessarily. Confirm owner-occupancy terms, legal use and any management agreement for the specific unit and project.

Do low HOA fees make a condo less expensive?

Not by themselves. Compare what dues cover, reserves, insurance, assessments and costs the owner pays directly.
